The Clinical Challenge

Why Standard Adult E-Prescribing Fails Pediatric Oncology

Trap 1 • The 10x Oral Syringe Overdose 0.3 mL vs 3.0 mL

Decimal Confusion on Concentrated Liquids

A 2-year-old toddler (12 kg) tapering Prednisolone (10 mg/mL) needs 3.0 mg = 0.30 mL. Entering 3.0 mL delivers 30 mg (a 10-fold overdose) because EHRs lack physical syringe visualization.

Trap 2 • Protocol Ceiling & Renal Blindness Max 2.00 mg Cap

Unclamped BSA Dosing & Post-SCT Renal Shifts

For a 15-year-old adolescent (60 kg, 166 cm → BSA 1.66 m²), raw Vincristine 1.5 mg/m² equals 2.49 mg — causing severe neurotoxicity unless hard-clamped at 2.00 mg.

Trap 3 • Belgian Reimbursement & Trial Silos HEM-iSmart & RIZIV

Convention vs. Chapter IV & Sponsor IMP Stock

Noxafil 40 mg/mL Suspension is only reimbursed via the Belgian Pediatric Oncology Convention, whereas 100 mg Tablets are covered under RIZIV Chapter IV (§4460100). Meanwhile, Ruxolitinib in HEM-iSmart must be locked from commercial Recip-e.

Trap 4 • The Kitchen-Table Crisis at Home Adherence & PK

Bitter Steroids, Crushed Tablets & Unverified Advice

Caregivers struggle to give bitter Medrol or oral chemo (!PURI). Without validated tips, parents may mix drugs into grapefruit juice (CYP3A4 toxicity) or warm milk (destroying Losec MUPS enteric coating).
